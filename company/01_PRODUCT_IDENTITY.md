# Product Identity Bible

## 1. Product Category

Review-to-Revenue AI creates a new category:

**AI Revenue Intelligence Platform for Customer Voice**

This is not a review analytics category. It is a revenue workflow category built on customer language.

The product reads customer voice sources such as reviews, inquiries, consultation notes, competitor complaints, and lead CSV data, then turns them into revenue actions a business can execute this week.

Category definition:

> AI software that converts unstructured customer language into prioritized follow-up, content, sales script, positioning, and outcome-tracking actions.

## 2. One-line Positioning

Primary positioning line:

> Turn customer voice into revenue actions.

Why this wins:

- It is broad enough to include reviews, leads, sales notes, inquiries, and competitor feedback.
- It keeps the product focused on action, not analysis.
- It does not trap the product inside CRM, reputation management, or report-generation language.
- It is simple enough for landing pages, demos, investor decks, and sales calls.

Candidate evaluation:

- "Turn customer voice into revenue actions."  
  Best primary line. Clear, durable, category-defining, and not limited to reviews.

- "Stop summarizing reviews. Start recovering revenue."  
  Strong campaign line. Useful for landing-page pain sections, but too narrow because the product is not only about reviews.

- "Every customer conversation hides revenue."  
  Good brand idea, but softer and less concrete than the primary line.

- "Know who to follow up with, what to send, and what to improve."  
  Strong explanatory subheadline. Excellent for demos, but too long as the main identity.

## 3. What We Are NOT

Review-to-Revenue AI is not a review summarizer.

It does not exist to produce generic summaries, sentiment labels, topic clusters, or prettier review reports.

Review-to-Revenue AI is not a CRM.

It does not try to replace pipeline management, contact records, sales stages, team permissions, or sales forecasting systems.

Review-to-Revenue AI is not a reputation management tool.

It does not focus on review replies, star ratings, listings, public reputation monitoring, or review acquisition campaigns.

Review-to-Revenue AI is not a generic AI report generator.

Reports are not the product. Reports are packaging for actions. The core product is the closed loop from customer language to revenue action to tracked outcome.

## 4. Core Customer

Primary ICP:

**Clinics / med spas**

Why this should be the first vertical:

- High-value consultations and procedures make recovered leads financially meaningful.
- Customer objections are often language-heavy: price, trust, downtime, proof, spouse approval, risk, expectations, and comparison.
- Reviews, DMs, consult notes, and lead follow-ups all influence booking decisions.
- Follow-up messages, content ideas, and sales script improvements can directly affect revenue.
- Small teams often lack a dedicated revenue operations function, so the product can create immediate leverage.

Secondary ICP:

**Wedding vendors**

Why this should be the second validation vertical:

- High-ticket, emotionally complex purchases.
- Couples compare vendors and pause when proof, package scope, pricing, timeline, or partner approval is unclear.
- Follow-up quality strongly affects booking outcomes.
- Content and sales scripts can be tied directly to real objections.

Verticals to keep as future expansion:

- Hotels / resorts: strong review volume and direct-booking potential, but less consistent lead-rescue workflow unless the property has active inquiry or reservation sales data.
- Franchise consultants: strong revenue fit, but narrower buyer pool and higher trust burden.
- Real estate: high value, but workflow complexity and compliance risk are higher.
- Home remodeling: strong objections and lead value, but project-specific quoting workflows may require deeper vertical customization.

## 5. Core Pain

Businesses already have the revenue clues. They are buried in customer language.

The painful problem:

- Leads go cold because follow-up happens too late or sounds generic.
- Reviews reveal objections, but teams treat them as feedback instead of sales intelligence.
- Follow-ups do not answer the specific concern that stopped the buyer.
- Content is created from guesses, not from repeated customer objections.
- Sales scripts do not adapt when customers keep raising the same trust barriers.
- Competitor weaknesses are visible in public complaints but rarely turned into positioning.
- No one tracks whether acting on these insights created replies, bookings, or won deals.

The business consequence:

> Teams keep losing revenue they could have recovered because customer language is not connected to action or outcome.

## 6. Core Promise

Within 30 seconds, Review-to-Revenue AI should answer:

- Who should I contact?
- What should I send?
- What content should I publish?
- What sales script should I improve?
- What competitor weakness can I use?
- What happened after I acted?

The promise is not "understand your customers."

The promise is:

> Know the next revenue action hiding inside customer language.

## 7. Product Loop

The product loop:

```text
Customer Voice
-> Revenue Signal
-> Revenue Action
-> Outcome Data
-> Better Recommendation
```

Customer Voice:

Raw input from reviews, inquiries, consultation notes, sales notes, competitor reviews, DMs, call notes, and lead CSV data.

Revenue Signal:

The commercially meaningful pattern found in that language: objection, buying trigger, trust barrier, competitor weakness, lead rescue opportunity, content gap, or script weakness.

Revenue Action:

The executable next step: send this follow-up, publish this content, adjust this script, address this objection, reposition against this competitor weakness, or rescue this lead.

Outcome Data:

Manual or future automated tracking of what happened after the action: copied, sent, replied, booked, won, lost, ignored, and estimated recovered revenue.

Better Recommendation:

Future intelligence layer that learns which actions, messages, objections, and content angles actually produce outcomes.

## 8. Naming Evaluation

### Review-to-Revenue AI

Recommendation: **Keep for now.**

Strengths:

- Clear transformation from customer feedback to commercial outcome.
- Strong enough for an MVP and early sales conversations.
- Keeps the current codebase, docs, and product history coherent.
- Good contrast against review summarizers.

Weakness:

- The word "Review" can make the product sound narrower than the intended customer voice platform.

Decision:

Keep `Review-to-Revenue AI` through validation. Define "review" broadly in messaging as customer voice, not only public reviews. Consider renaming only after the first paying ICP is clear.

### Revenue Signal AI

Recommendation: Do not rename now.

Strengths:

- Strong B2B feel.
- Matches the dashboard concept.

Weaknesses:

- Signal is intermediate, not the final value.
- Could sound like analytics rather than action.

### Customer Signal AI

Recommendation: Do not use.

Strengths:

- Broad and customer-centric.

Weaknesses:

- Generic.
- Does not communicate revenue or action strongly enough.

### Voice-to-Revenue

Recommendation: Keep as a future rename candidate.

Strengths:

- Best long-term category name if the product expands beyond reviews.
- Directly supports the Customer Voice -> Revenue Action loop.

Weaknesses:

- Slightly less concrete for early users who understand reviews faster than "voice."
- May need more explanation in sales copy.

### Revenue Lens

Recommendation: Do not use.

Strengths:

- Short.
- Connects to the old lens concept.

Weaknesses:

- Too close to Review Lens.
- Lens implies analysis, not action.

### RevenueOS

Recommendation: Do not use now.

Strengths:

- Ambitious.

Weaknesses:

- Too broad for the MVP.
- Sounds like CRM, sales operating system, or revenue operations suite.
- Creates expectations the product should not carry yet.

Final naming recommendation:

> Keep Review-to-Revenue AI for the MVP. Revisit Voice-to-Revenue only after vertical validation proves that the product is clearly broader than reviews.

## 10. Product Voice

The product voice should be:

- Sharp
- Commercial
- B2B
- Specific
- Evidence-aware
- Action-oriented
- Calmly confident

The product voice should not be:

- Hype-driven
- Cute
- Vague
- Overly friendly
- Generic AI copy
- Dashboard jargon
- Consultant-speak without operational clarity

Writing rules:

- Say "revenue action" more often than "insight."
- Say "customer voice" more often than "review data."
- Say "what to send" instead of "AI-generated communication."
- Say "what happened after you acted" instead of "performance analytics."
- Avoid promising guaranteed revenue.
- Avoid sounding like a CRM replacement.

## 11. Investor-grade Summary

Review-to-Revenue AI is an AI Revenue Intelligence Platform for Customer Voice. The product converts unstructured customer language from reviews, inquiries, sales notes, competitor complaints, and lead data into weekly revenue actions: who to follow up with, what to send, what content to publish, what sales script to improve, and what competitor weakness to use. Unlike review summarizers, CRMs, or generic AI report tools, Review-to-Revenue AI closes the loop between customer language, recommended action, and outcome data, creating a path toward revenue intelligence that learns which messages and actions actually recover revenue.
