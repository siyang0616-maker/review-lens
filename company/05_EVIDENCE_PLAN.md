# Evidence Plan & Kill Criteria

Stage 5B

## Purpose

This document defines what evidence must exist before Review-to-Revenue AI invests months into building a larger SaaS product.

The rule for this stage:

> Assume nothing. Every major assumption must become validated or rejected.

This is not a product improvement plan.
This is not a UI plan.
This is not the Master PRD.

It is the evidence gate before the Master PRD.

## Section 3: 90-Day Validation Roadmap

### Week 1: Prospect List And Offer Clarity

Goal:

- Build a focused list of Franchise / Business Consulting prospects and define one validation offer.

Experiment:

- Create a list of 50 prospects.
- Write 3 founder-led outreach messages.
- Use one concrete offer: "Send 10-20 anonymized lead notes. Get 3 revenue leaks and 3 follow-up actions."

Expected learning:

- Whether the target list is reachable and whether the offer is understandable.

Success criteria:

- 50 qualified prospects identified.
- 3 outreach variants ready.
- 10 warm or semi-warm prospects prioritized.

### Week 2: Pain Interviews

Goal:

- Validate that lead leakage and repeated objections are real weekly pains.

Experiment:

- Run 10 interviews.
- Ask about recent stale leads, current follow-up behavior, repeated objections, and current tools.

Expected learning:

- Whether the pain exists without pitching the product.

Success criteria:

- 5+ prospects describe recent stale leads.
- 5+ identify repeated objections.
- 3+ agree to share anonymized notes.

### Week 3: Data Sharing Test

Goal:

- Validate whether prospects will share customer conversations.

Experiment:

- Ask 15 prospects for 10-20 anonymized notes, inquiries, or consultation snippets.
- Offer cleanup help for messy data.

Expected learning:

- Whether data access is a real blocker.

Success criteria:

- 5+ usable submissions.
- Document every refusal reason.

### Week 4: First Free Diagnostics

Goal:

- Validate whether real data produces useful actions.

Experiment:

- Deliver 5 free Revenue Leak Diagnostics manually.
- Include 3 leaks, 3 actions, and 1 sample follow-up message.

Expected learning:

- Whether the output feels specific, trusted, and actionable.

Success criteria:

- 4+ recipients identify at least one action they would use.
- 3+ accept a review call.

### Week 5: Paid Report Offer

Goal:

- Validate willingness to pay.

Experiment:

- Offer the paid Revenue Action Report at KRW 390,000 / $299 after diagnostic calls.

Expected learning:

- Whether free value converts into money.

Success criteria:

- 5+ paid offers made.
- 1+ paid report sold.

### Week 6: Paid Report Delivery

Goal:

- Validate that paid delivery creates deeper value than a free diagnostic.

Experiment:

- Deliver paid reports.
- Track delivery time, custom work, reusable patterns, and buyer reaction.

Expected learning:

- Whether report delivery is productizable or just custom consulting.

Success criteria:

- 1-2 paid reports delivered.
- 50%+ of report structure is reusable.
- Buyer agrees to execute at least one action.

### Week 7: Action Execution Test

Goal:

- Validate whether customers act.

Experiment:

- Ask every report/diagnostic recipient to send one follow-up, use one script line, or draft one content piece.

Expected learning:

- Whether recommendations move from reading to behavior.

Success criteria:

- 3+ actions executed.
- 2+ follow-up messages sent.

### Week 8: Outcome Recording Test

Goal:

- Validate manual outcome tracking.

Experiment:

- Follow up 7 days after executed actions and record replied, booked, won, lost, ignored, or qualitative improvement.

Expected learning:

- Whether customers will report what happened.

Success criteria:

- 2+ credible outcomes recorded.
- 50%+ of action executors respond to outcome follow-up.

### Week 9: Repeat Workflow Test

Goal:

- Validate whether customers want a second cycle.

Experiment:

- Offer a second weekly refresh using new notes or outcomes from the first action.

Expected learning:

- Whether this is recurring or one-time value.

Success criteria:

- 2+ customers accept a second refresh.
- 1+ customer asks what ongoing help would cost.

### Week 10: Setup Package Test

Goal:

- Validate whether customers want help turning the report into a system.

Experiment:

- Offer a scoped setup package to paid report buyers or strongest diagnostic users.

Expected learning:

- Whether setup is a real bridge to recurring value.

Success criteria:

- 1 setup purchase or serious written commitment.
- Clear objections documented if no purchase.

### Week 11: Monthly Refresh Test

Goal:

- Validate early recurring revenue.

Experiment:

- Offer a monthly action refresh, not full SaaS, to customers who executed actions.

Expected learning:

- Whether the weekly/monthly habit can be monetized.

Success criteria:

- 1 paid monthly refresh commitment or 2 serious recurring-intent signals.

### Week 12: Decision Week

Goal:

- Decide continue, pivot, or stop.

Experiment:

- Review scoreboard.
- Compare results against kill criteria.
- Decide whether Stage 6 should be Master PRD, pivot plan, or shutdown/post-mortem.

Expected learning:

- Whether evidence supports building.

Success criteria:

- 2+ paid reports.
- 8+ usable data submissions.
- 3+ actions executed.
- 2+ outcomes recorded.
- 1+ recurring/setup signal.

## Section 5: Kill Criteria

The company should stop, pivot, or refuse to build more product when these thresholds are hit.

### Stop Criteria

Stop the current product direction if any of these are true:

1. Fewer than 5 interviews are booked after 50 qualified founder-led outreach attempts.
2. Fewer than 3 prospects share usable data after 50 asks.
3. 0 paid reports are sold after 10 free diagnostics and 8 direct paid offers.
4. 0 customers execute a recommended action after 5 delivered diagnostics/reports.
5. 0 credible outcomes are recorded after 10 executed recommendations.
6. 50%+ of prospects say ChatGPT is good enough after seeing output.
7. Every paid delivery requires mostly custom founder judgment by the third paid report.
8. No customer asks for a second refresh, setup, or recurring help after successful paid delivery.

### Pivot Criteria

Pivot, narrow, or change the offer if:

1. Pain is real but customers will not share data.
   - Pivot toward founder-led private audit calls or customer-controlled anonymization.
2. Customers share data but will not pay.
   - Pivot pricing, buyer, or urgency framing.
3. Reports sell but nobody executes actions.
   - Pivot from reports to done-with-you execution.
4. Follow-up messages are used but content/script suggestions are ignored.
   - Narrow the product to lead rescue and follow-up.
5. Franchise consultants reject the offer but clinics, wedding vendors, or home remodeling prospects show stronger pull.
   - Change beachhead only with evidence, not intuition.
6. Customers want custom advisory but not recurring software.
   - Treat it as a service business until repeatability appears.

### Continue Criteria

Continue toward Stage 6 only if the 90-day sprint produces:

1. 20 interviews completed.
2. 8+ usable customer voice submissions.
3. 10 free diagnostics delivered.
4. 2+ paid reports sold.
5. 3+ recommended actions executed.
6. 2+ credible outcomes recorded.
7. 1+ setup, monthly refresh, or recurring-help commitment.
8. 1 clear repeated objection taxonomy for Franchise / Business Consulting.

## Section 7: Customer Interview Script

Interview rule:

> Do not pitch first. Do not ask if they like the idea. Ask about recent behavior.

### Opening

1. Can you walk me through how new leads usually come in?
2. What happens after someone asks about price, risk, payback, timing, or comparison?
3. Can you describe the last few leads that went cold?
4. What did you send them after they stopped replying?
5. How do you decide who deserves a second or third follow-up?

### Current Pain

6. Which objections repeat most often in your conversations?
7. What questions do prospects ask before they trust you?
8. Where do your sales conversations usually get stuck?
9. What content do you wish you had when a prospect hesitates?
10. What part of follow-up feels most inconsistent right now?

### Current Workflow

11. Where do you keep lead notes, consultation notes, and message history?
12. Who reviews those notes today?
13. How often do you revisit old leads?
14. What tools do you use for follow-up now?
15. What happens to objections after a consultation ends?

### Data Access

16. If you removed names and phone numbers, would you be comfortable sharing 10-20 real lead notes?
17. What would make you uncomfortable sharing those notes?
18. What format would be easiest: pasted notes, spreadsheet, screenshots summarized into text, or call notes?
19. How messy is the data usually?
20. Who would need to approve sharing anonymized notes?

### Value Test

21. If I returned the top 3 revenue leaks from those notes, what would make that useful?
22. What would make it useless?
23. What would you need to see before paying for a deeper report?
24. What would one recovered consultation be worth to you?
25. What result would make you want this every month?

### Execution Test

26. If you received one specific follow-up message, would you send it yourself?
27. What would stop you from sending it?
28. Who on your team would actually use the message?
29. Would you be willing to tell me what happened 7 days later?
30. What outcome would count as success for you?

### Competitive / ChatGPT Test

31. Have you used ChatGPT or another AI tool for follow-up or content?
32. What worked?
33. What felt generic or risky?
34. What would a tool need to do better than ChatGPT for you to pay?
35. What existing tool would you compare this to?

### Closing

36. Can you send 10-20 anonymized notes for a free diagnostic?
37. If the diagnostic is useful, are you open to reviewing a paid report option?
38. Is there anyone else in this market I should speak with?

### Questions To Avoid

Do not ask:

- Would you use this product?
- Do you like the idea?
- Would you pay someday?
- Should we build a dashboard?
- Do you want AI?
- Which features do you want?

## Section 8: ChatGPT Risk

### Why Customers Might Use ChatGPT Instead

Customers may simply use ChatGPT because:

- It is already available.
- It feels free or cheap.
- They can paste notes manually.
- They can ask for follow-up messages, content ideas, or script improvements.
- They may not care about a separate workflow.
- They may not trust a new SaaS vendor with customer data.
- They may not see enough difference between a report and a prompt.

This is the most dangerous product risk because the first MVP is text in, text out.

### How To Prove Review-to-Revenue AI Creates Value Beyond A Prompt

The product must prove at least one of these differences:

1. Better diagnosis:
   - It finds the real objection pattern faster than a generic prompt.
2. Better evidence:
   - Every recommendation is tied to specific customer language.
3. Better prioritization:
   - It tells the user which action matters first.
4. Better vertical judgment:
   - It understands Franchise / Business Consulting objections better than generic AI.
5. Better execution:
   - It produces messages the customer actually sends.
6. Better outcome loop:
   - It asks what happened and improves the next recommendation.
7. Better packaging:
   - It creates a report buyers trust enough to pay for.

### ChatGPT Comparison Experiment

Run this experiment with 5 prospects:

1. Ask each prospect for the same anonymized data.
2. Generate one output using a generic ChatGPT-style prompt.
3. Generate one Review-to-Revenue diagnostic using the vertical framework.
4. Blind or semi-blind compare:
   - Which is more specific?
   - Which would you act on?
   - Which feels safer to send?
   - Which would you pay for?
   - Which found something you had missed?

Success:

- 4 out of 5 prospects choose Review-to-Revenue as more actionable.
- 3 out of 5 say they would pay for the Review-to-Revenue version.

Kill:

- 3 out of 5 say generic ChatGPT is good enough.
- Prospects copy the prompt and lose interest in the product.

## Section 9: Go / No-Go Framework

### Continue

Continue to Stage 6 Master PRD only if:

- The first beachhead produces clear pain.
- Prospects share usable customer voice data.
- Free diagnostics create concrete action value.
- Paid reports sell.
- Customers execute at least some actions.
- Customers report at least some outcomes.
- At least one customer wants setup, recurring refresh, or weekly help.

Minimum continue threshold:

```text
20 interviews
8 usable data submissions
10 free diagnostics
2 paid reports
3 executed actions
2 recorded outcomes
1 recurring/setup signal
```

### Pivot

Pivot if evidence shows one part works but the current model does not.

Pivot examples:

- If data sharing fails, pivot to guided audit calls or stricter anonymization.
- If diagnostics work but reports do not sell, pivot to done-with-you action sessions.
- If follow-up messages work but reports do not, narrow to lead rescue.
- If franchise consulting is weak but another vertical shows stronger pull, change beachhead.
- If customers pay for services but not software, stay service-led until repeatability is proven.

### Stop

Stop if:

- Prospects do not care.
- Prospects do not share data.
- Prospects do not pay.
- Customers do not execute actions.
- Outcomes cannot be collected.
- ChatGPT is perceived as enough.
- The founder must personally customize every deliverable.
- No recurring behavior appears after 90 days.

Stopping is not failure. It prevents months of building a product around a belief customers did not validate.

## Section 10: Recommended Stage 6

Recommended Stage 6:

> Master PRD, only if Stage 5B evidence passes the continue threshold.

Stage 6 should prepare the Master PRD only after the evidence sprint answers:

- Who is the exact first buyer?
- What data will they actually share?
- Which output do they pay for?
- Which action do they execute?
- Which outcome do they report?
- Which workflow repeats?
- Which language creates conversion?
- Which parts should be product, and which parts should remain service?

If the evidence threshold is not met, Stage 6 should not be a Master PRD.

Instead, Stage 6 should be one of:

- Pivot Plan.
- Narrower Offer Plan.
- Service-First Delivery Plan.
- Shutdown/Post-Mortem.

Final Stage 5B rule:

> Evidence first. Then product.
