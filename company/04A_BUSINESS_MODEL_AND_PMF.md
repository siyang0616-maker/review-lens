# Business Model & PMF Blueprint

## Purpose

This document defines how Review-to-Revenue AI should make money, who it should sell to first, how it should reach product-market fit, and how it should expand.

This is not a product build plan.

This is the commercial blueprint before the Master PRD.

Core strategic rule:

> Do not start by selling software. Start by selling recovered revenue clarity.

## 1. Business Model Options

### Model 1: One-time Revenue Action Report

Target customer:

- Founder-led service businesses.
- Franchise / business consultants.
- Consultants, agencies, clinics, wedding vendors, and high-consideration service teams with messy leads, notes, reviews, and objections.

Pricing:

- KRW: 290,000-790,000 per report.
- USD: $199-$599 per report.

Pros:

- Easy to understand.
- Fast to sell.
- Does not require login, integrations, or workflow change.
- Good for validating willingness to pay.
- Lets the founder learn customer language manually.
- Creates proof assets for later SaaS conversion.

Cons:

- Lower retention.
- Manual delivery can become service-heavy.
- Customers may see it as a consulting report, not software.
- Repeat purchase must be created through weekly or monthly action updates.

Validation difficulty:

- Low.

Scalability:

- Medium at first, low if fully manual, high later if report generation becomes productized.

Best use case:

- First paid validation.
- Early customer interviews.
- Learning vertical-specific objections.
- Building before/after proof.

### Model 2: Weekly SaaS Subscription

Target customer:

- Small revenue teams that need a weekly action board.
- Franchise consultants, clinic sales teams, wedding vendors, agencies, and local service businesses with recurring leads.

Pricing:

- KRW: 99,000-490,000 per month.
- USD: $79-$399 per month.

Pros:

- Recurring revenue.
- Supports outcome tracking and data flywheel.
- Aligns with the weekly revenue action workflow.
- Can become the core scalable business model.

Cons:

- Harder to sell before trust is built.
- Requires ongoing usage.
- Needs clear weekly habit formation.
- Without setup, customers may not upload enough useful data.

Validation difficulty:

- Medium to high.

Scalability:

- High if onboarding, templates, and recurring workflow work.

Best use case:

- After a customer has seen value from a free diagnostic, one-time report, or setup package.

### Model 3: Done-for-you Setup Service

Target customer:

- Customers with valuable customer language but poor process.
- Franchise / business consultants with lead notes, message history, sales objections, and content gaps.
- Clinics, agencies, or vendors that want the result but do not want to configure anything.

Pricing:

- KRW: 1,500,000-4,900,000 setup package.
- USD: $1,200-$3,900 setup package.

Pros:

- Higher ticket.
- Stronger trust bridge between report and SaaS.
- Lets the founder build the first playbooks manually.
- Creates tailored datasets, objection taxonomies, content angles, and script examples.
- Can include onboarding into monthly subscription.

Cons:

- Service-heavy.
- Harder to scale without templates.
- Delivery quality depends on founder time.
- Scope creep risk.

Validation difficulty:

- Medium.

Scalability:

- Medium. It scales only after playbooks and delivery templates are standardized.

Best use case:

- Converting report buyers into recurring subscribers.
- Learning what customers cannot do alone.
- Creating the first vertical playbook.

### Model 4: Agency / Consultant Toolkit

Target customer:

- Marketing agencies.
- Franchise consultants.
- Sales consultants.
- Revenue consultants.
- Local business consultants who serve multiple clients.

Pricing:

- KRW: 490,000-1,500,000 per month per consultant or agency seat.
- USD: $399-$1,200 per month.
- Optional client workspace add-ons later.

Pros:

- One buyer can bring multiple end clients.
- Strong distribution leverage.
- Consultants understand the value of reports, scripts, and action plans.
- Can create a white-label or co-branded services layer later.

Cons:

- Agencies may use the output as a service deliverable and avoid product dependency.
- Needs better export, report, and client presentation workflows.
- May pull the product toward agency tooling too early.

Validation difficulty:

- Medium.

Scalability:

- High if the toolkit becomes repeatable and client delivery is standardized.

Best use case:

- Second-stage distribution after the founder validates the report and setup workflow directly.

### Model 5: Later Enterprise Platform

Target customer:

- Multi-location brands.
- Large franchise networks.
- Hospitality groups.
- Clinic chains.
- Real estate brokerages.
- Home service networks.

Pricing:

- KRW: 30,000,000+ annually.
- USD: $25,000+ annually.
- Expansion by seats, locations, volume, or integrations.

Pros:

- Large contracts.
- Stronger data flywheel.
- More defensible if integrations and playbooks compound.
- Fits the investor narrative.

Cons:

- Long sales cycles.
- Requires security, admin, permissions, integrations, support, reporting, and procurement readiness.
- Too early for MVP.
- Can distract from founder-led PMF.

Validation difficulty:

- High.

Scalability:

- High only after product, data, integrations, and proof are mature.

Best use case:

- Later expansion after repeated proof in one vertical.

## 2. Recommended Starting Model

Recommended starting sequence:

```text
Free diagnostic report
-> Paid one-time Revenue Action Report
-> Done-for-you setup service
-> Monthly SaaS subscription
```

Why this is the best first model:

- It matches the current product maturity.
- It does not require a full SaaS system before validation.
- It lets the founder sell value before selling software.
- It uses founder domain knowledge instead of fighting for generic SaaS attention.
- It creates paid learning loops quickly.
- It keeps future SaaS grounded in real customer language and real objections.

Starting offer:

> Free Revenue Leak Diagnostic

Paid first offer:

> Revenue Action Report

Setup offer:

> Revenue Action System Setup

Subscription offer:

> Weekly Revenue Action Board

Recommended first funnel:

```text
Founder-led outreach
-> Free diagnostic
-> 20-minute review call
-> Paid report offer
-> Setup package offer
-> Monthly subscription
```

What to avoid:

- Do not lead with "subscribe to our SaaS."
- Do not sell "AI analysis."
- Do not offer broad CRM replacement.
- Do not promise guaranteed recovered revenue.

## 3. First Beachhead

### Franchise / Business Consulting

Recommendation:

> First beachhead.

Why this should be first:

- Founder has domain knowledge.
- Founder understands the customer language, content workflow, objections, and lead flow.
- Leads are high-value.
- Objections are explicit and repeated: investment amount, payback period, risk, location, brand comparison, spouse/family approval, operator workload, trust, and proof.
- The workflow naturally needs follow-up messages, content ideas, script improvements, and lead rescue.
- Manual report delivery is acceptable at first.
- The first customers can validate whether the action layer matters before the product becomes a full SaaS.

What data they likely have:

- Sales notes.
- Lead CSVs.
- Consultation notes.
- KakaoTalk / SMS / DM snippets.
- Blog comments or form inquiries.
- Competitor reviews or competitor complaint screenshots.
- Content performance observations.

Why this beats other first markets:

- Hotels and clinics may be commercially attractive but have more platform competition, operational complexity, or compliance concerns.
- Wedding and home remodeling are strong but require more discovery.
- Franchise / business consulting has the best founder-market fit and fastest learning cycle.

### Hotels / Resorts

Strengths:

- High review volume.
- Clear guest feedback.
- Strong demo story.

Weaknesses:

- Crowded with hospitality review and guest feedback platforms.
- Direct revenue action loop may require booking inquiry data, not only reviews.

Recommendation:

- Keep as demo and later vertical, not first beachhead.

### Wedding Vendors

Strengths:

- High emotional stakes.
- Partner approval, proof, package clarity, price, and timing objections are common.
- Follow-up quality matters.

Weaknesses:

- Fragmented market.
- Smaller operators may lack structured data.

Recommendation:

- Good second or third validation market after franchise consulting.

### Clinics / Med Spas

Strengths:

- High-value consultations.
- Strong objections around price, trust, downtime, proof, expectations, and risk.
- High value from better follow-up and content.

Weaknesses:

- Medical-adjacent claims require careful messaging.
- More compliance sensitivity.

Recommendation:

- Best second commercial vertical once the core workflow is validated.

### Real Estate

Strengths:

- High-value leads.
- Follow-up and trust matter.

Weaknesses:

- Compliance, brokerage workflows, CRM saturation, and market cycles can complicate validation.

Recommendation:

- Later expansion.

### Home Remodeling

Strengths:

- High ticket.
- Objections around trust, quote clarity, timeline, materials, proof, and budget.

Weaknesses:

- Project complexity varies.
- Data can be scattered and inconsistent.

Recommendation:

- Strong later vertical after playbooks mature.

Final beachhead decision:

> Start with Franchise / Business Consulting.

Expansion candidate after first proof:

> Clinics / med spas.

## 4. ICP

First ideal customer profile:

> A founder-led franchise or business consulting operator who sells high-ticket consulting, franchise matching, business acquisition support, startup advisory, or opportunity consulting and receives recurring inbound leads from content, ads, referrals, search, blogs, or social channels.

Who they are:

- Founder, principal consultant, or small consulting team.
- Revenue depends on consultations, lead follow-up, and trust-building content.
- They personally understand the sales process but do not have a systematic way to extract objections from customer language.

What they sell:

- Franchise consulting.
- Business opportunity consulting.
- Startup or self-employment advisory.
- Franchise brand comparison.
- Location or market opportunity guidance.
- High-ticket advisory packages or commission-driven consulting.

How they get leads:

- Blog posts.
- Search traffic.
- YouTube or short-form content.
- Direct inquiries.
- KakaoTalk / SMS / email / DM.
- Referral.
- Lead forms.
- Paid ads.
- Franchise-related content funnels.

What data they have:

- Lead spreadsheets.
- Consultation notes.
- Message history.
- Form submissions.
- Sales notes.
- Objection patterns.
- Blog comments.
- Competitor complaints.
- Customer questions.

What pain they feel weekly:

- Leads go cold after asking about cost, payback, risk, or family approval.
- Follow-ups are inconsistent.
- The same objections repeat, but scripts do not improve.
- Content topics are guessed rather than based on real objections.
- The founder knows there is signal in the conversations but does not have time to organize it.
- Marketing creates leads, but sales follow-up does not recover enough of them.

Why they would pay:

- One recovered consultation or deal can justify the cost.
- They already understand the value of better objection handling.
- They need concrete follow-up messages and content topics, not abstract analytics.
- A report can produce immediate usable assets.
- The founder can see their own customer language reflected in the output.

Why they would not pay:

- They believe ChatGPT can do it if they paste notes manually.
- They do not have enough leads or data.
- They do not follow up consistently.
- They want guaranteed revenue.
- They do not trust AI-generated sales advice.
- They are too early or too small to care about systematic revenue recovery.

Disqualifiers:

- No recurring lead flow.
- No written customer language.
- No willingness to share anonymized notes.
- No follow-up process.
- Only wants a one-time curiosity report.

## 7. PMF Validation Plan

Timeframe:

> 14 days.

Goal:

Validate whether Franchise / Business Consulting customers will share customer voice data, accept a diagnostic, pay for a Revenue Action Report, and consider a setup or subscription offer.

### Targets

Interviews:

- 15-20 founder-led conversations.

Free diagnostic reports:

- 8-10 delivered.

Paid offers:

- 5-8 made.

Paid conversions:

- Minimum success: 2 paid reports.
- Strong success: 3+ paid reports or 1 setup package.
- Excellent success: 3+ paid reports plus 1 subscription or setup commitment.

### 14-day schedule

Days 1-2:

- Build prospect list.
- Prepare outreach message.
- Prepare free diagnostic intake format.
- Select 20-30 target prospects.

Days 3-5:

- Send founder-led outreach.
- Book interviews.
- Ask for anonymized customer voice data.

Days 6-9:

- Deliver free diagnostics.
- Run 20-minute review calls.
- Identify strongest pain patterns.

Days 10-12:

- Offer paid Revenue Action Report.
- Test pricing at KRW 390,000 / $299.
- Offer setup package to strongest-fit prospects.

Days 13-14:

- Review conversion.
- Document objections.
- Decide whether to continue, narrow, reposition, or pause.

### Success criteria

Proceed if:

- 10+ prospects agree the pain is real.
- 6+ share usable customer voice data.
- 5+ say the diagnostic showed something actionable.
- 2+ pay for a report.
- At least 1 customer asks for ongoing weekly or monthly help.
- The founder can identify repeated objection patterns across multiple prospects.

### Failure criteria

Pause or reposition if:

- Prospects will not share data.
- Prospects say the diagnostic is interesting but not urgent.
- No one is willing to pay for the report.
- Users only want generic ChatGPT prompts.
- The output does not produce clear action.
- There is no repeatable weekly use case.

### What to ask

Ask:

- What happens to leads after they ask about price, risk, or payback?
- Which objections repeat most often?
- How do you decide who to follow up with?
- What do you send when someone goes cold?
- What content do you wish you had during follow-up?
- Which sales conversation keeps getting stuck?
- What would make this report worth paying for?
- What would make you use this every week?
- What would you need to see to buy a setup package?

### What not to ask

Do not ask:

- Would you use an AI tool like this?
- Do you like this idea?
- Would you pay someday?
- What features do you want?
- Should this be a dashboard?
- Should this integrate with your CRM?
- Would you prefer OpenAI or another model?

Reason:

> Ask about current behavior, painful moments, data availability, willingness to pay, and next action. Do not ask for speculative product opinions.

Best for:

- Warm prospects.
- Consultants posting content.
- Operators publicly discussing lead quality.

DM structure:

1. Mention the specific business context.
2. Name a likely objection.
3. Offer a free diagnostic from anonymized notes.
4. Avoid pitching software first.

### Cold email

Subject ideas:

- "Your old leads may already explain why they went cold"
- "Free diagnostic: which franchise leads should you follow up with?"
- "I can turn your consultation notes into 3 revenue actions"

Email CTA:

> Reply with 5-10 anonymized lead notes, and I will send back a short Revenue Leak Diagnostic.

### Free report offer

The free diagnostic should feel useful but incomplete.

It should include:

- 3 revenue leaks.
- 3 action opportunities.
- 1 sample follow-up message.
- 1 paid report recommendation.

### Demo call

Call structure:

1. Confirm the customer's current follow-up process.
2. Walk through the diagnostic.
3. Ask which action they would actually use.
4. Ask what one recovered lead is worth.
5. Offer the paid report or setup package.

### Paid conversion

First paid offer:

> I can turn this into a full Revenue Action Report with follow-up messages, content ideas, script improvements, and a 7-day action plan.

Price anchor:

> If one recovered consultation is worth more than this report, it is worth testing once.

## 9. Revenue Loop

The product creates revenue through this loop:

```text
Customer voice data
-> Revenue actions
-> Follow-up/content/script execution
-> Outcome tracking
-> Proof of recovered value
-> Subscription/setup upsell
```

Step 1: Customer voice data

- Customer shares reviews, lead notes, sales notes, competitor complaints, or consultation messages.

Step 2: Revenue actions

- Product identifies who to contact, what to send, what content to publish, and what script to improve.

Step 3: Execution

- Customer sends follow-ups, publishes content, changes scripts, or creates trust assets.

Step 4: Outcome tracking

- Customer marks whether actions were sent, replied to, booked, won, lost, or ignored.

Step 5: Proof of recovered value

- Product shows conversations recovered, bookings created, deals influenced, or objections reduced.

Step 6: Upsell

- Customer buys setup, subscription, or ongoing action reports because the value is tied to action and outcome.

## 10. Data Flywheel

The product improves through this loop:

```text
More customer voice
-> Better objection taxonomy
-> Better actions
-> More outcomes
-> Better playbooks
-> Stronger vertical intelligence
```

More customer voice:

- More notes, reviews, inquiries, and competitor complaints reveal repeated patterns.

Better objection taxonomy:

- The product learns the language of price resistance, risk, proof gaps, trust barriers, comparison, urgency, and decision committees.

Better actions:

- Follow-ups, content ideas, script suggestions, and positioning become sharper.

More outcomes:

- Tracking shows which actions create replies, bookings, and wins.

Better playbooks:

- Repeated patterns become vertical-specific playbooks.

Stronger vertical intelligence:

- The product becomes harder to copy because it knows what works in each vertical, not just how to summarize text.

## 11. Expansion Strategy

Recommended expansion order:

```text
Franchise / business consulting
-> Clinics / med spas
-> Wedding vendors
-> Home remodeling
-> Hotels / resorts
-> Real estate
```

Why this order:

1. Franchise / business consulting has the strongest founder-market fit and fastest learning loop.
2. Clinics / med spas have high-value consultations and strong objection patterns.
3. Wedding vendors have emotionally complex, high-ticket buying decisions with clear follow-up pain.
4. Home remodeling has strong trust, quote, proof, and timeline objections.
5. Hotels / resorts have strong review data but more crowded incumbent platforms.
6. Real estate has high value but heavier compliance, CRM saturation, and workflow complexity.

Alternative order if early traction changes:

```text
Franchise / business consulting
-> Wedding vendors
-> Clinics / med spas
-> Home remodeling
-> Hotels / resorts
-> Real estate
```

Decision rule:

> Expand to the vertical where customers share data fastest, pay for reports fastest, and execute recommendations most consistently.

## 13. Key Risks

### Risk 1: Customers see it as a ChatGPT wrapper.

Why:

- The first MVP may look like text in, text out.

Mitigation:

- Ground every output in customer evidence.
- Use vertical objection taxonomies.
- Track outcomes.
- Package outputs as revenue actions, not generic AI copy.
- Show before/after examples.

### Risk 2: Customers do not upload or share data.

Why:

- Customer notes may feel sensitive.
- Data may be messy.
- Users may not know what to provide.

Mitigation:

- Start with anonymized snippets.
- Offer a simple intake template.
- Accept pasted notes before integrations.
- Provide examples of good input.
- Offer done-for-you cleanup in setup package.

### Risk 3: One-time report sells but retention is weak.

Why:

- Customers may buy once, use the report, and not return.

Mitigation:

- Include a 7-day action plan.
- Schedule a follow-up outcome review.
- Offer monthly action refresh.
- Tie subscription to weekly pipeline review.
- Track replies, bookings, and wins.

### Risk 4: Too many verticals.

Why:

- Broad vertical focus creates generic output and weak positioning.

Mitigation:

- Start with Franchise / Business Consulting.
- Build one objection taxonomy deeply.
- Expand only after repeatable paid demand.
- Keep other verticals as demo or later playbooks.

### Risk 5: Competitors copy action suggestions.

Why:

- Review, CRM, and sales tools can add AI-generated suggestions.

Mitigation:

- Build outcome learning.
- Build vertical playbooks.
- Own the weekly action workflow.
- Make recommendations evidence-backed and outcome-aware.

### Risk 6: Outcome tracking depends on user discipline.

Why:

- Users may not mark sent, replied, booked, or won.

Mitigation:

- Keep statuses lightweight.
- Make outcome tracking part of the review call.
- Include outcome review in setup and subscription.
- Later add import/export or integration only after workflow validation.

### Risk 7: Customers want guaranteed revenue.

Why:

- Revenue language can create unrealistic expectations.

Mitigation:

- Frame revenue numbers as estimates.
- Sell "better action and recovered conversations," not guaranteed sales.
- Use proof from actual outcomes only.

### Risk 8: Founder-led delivery becomes too service-heavy.

Why:

- Early setup and reports may depend heavily on founder judgment.

Mitigation:

- Document every report pattern.
- Convert repeated work into templates.
- Build vertical playbooks.
- Use service delivery to design product, not as the final business.
