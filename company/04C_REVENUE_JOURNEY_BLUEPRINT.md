# Revenue Journey Blueprint

## Purpose

This document defines how a stranger becomes a lead, tries Review-to-Revenue AI, receives value, pays, upgrades, retains, and refers others.

This is not just a customer journey.

This is the Revenue Journey.

Core principle:

> The journey should sell recovered revenue clarity before it sells software.

## 1. Revenue Journey Overview

Full journey:

```text
First touch
-> Landing page
-> Live demo
-> Free diagnostic report
-> Paid Revenue Action Report
-> Done-for-you setup
-> Monthly SaaS
-> Outcome tracking
-> Retention
-> Referral
```

The journey should feel lightweight at the start and become more valuable as the customer shares more customer voice data and tracks outcomes.

The user should never feel forced to replace a CRM, install a heavy system, or commit to a subscription before seeing value.

Journey promise:

> Bring us the customer conversations you already have. We will show who to contact, what to send, what to publish, what script to improve, and what happened after you acted.

Commercial logic:

```text
Clear pain
-> Fast demo proof
-> Free personalized diagnostic
-> Paid report
-> Setup help
-> Weekly action habit
-> Outcome proof
-> Subscription and referrals
```

## 2. Journey Stages

### A. Awareness

User mindset:

- "I am losing leads, but I am not sure where the leak is."
- "I have notes, messages, reviews, or inquiries, but no system for turning them into action."
- "I do not need another dashboard."

Pain felt:

- Leads go cold.
- Follow-ups are generic.
- The same objections repeat.
- Content does not answer real buyer concerns.
- Sales scripts are not improving.

Message they need to hear:

> Review tools tell you what customers said. Review-to-Revenue AI tells you what to do next.

Action we want them to take:

- Click through to the landing page.
- Watch a demo clip.
- Request a free diagnostic.

Product asset shown:

- Short comparison post.
- Founder-led LinkedIn/blog post.
- Before/after example.
- Sample revenue action card.
- Mini case study or diagnostic teaser.

Conversion goal:

- Landing page visit or direct free diagnostic request.

Failure risk:

- The product sounds like another AI summary tool.

Mitigation:

- Lead with specific actions: who to contact, what to send, what to publish, what script to improve.
- Avoid "AI-powered insights" language.

### B. Landing Page Visit

User mindset:

- "Is this relevant to my business?"
- "Is this a review tool, CRM, or report generator?"
- "Can this help me recover leads?"

Pain felt:

- They see the problem but need proof that the product is different.

Message they need to hear:

> Not another review summary. A revenue action platform for customer voice.

Action we want them to take:

- Start the live demo.
- View a sample report.
- Request a free Revenue Action Report from their own data.

Product asset shown:

- Hero promise.
- Revenue action board preview.
- Comparison section.
- Sample report.
- First beachhead example for Franchise / Business Consulting.

Conversion goal:

- Live demo click or free report request.

Failure risk:

- Landing page feels generic.

Mitigation:

- Use beachhead-specific examples.
- Show action cards, not abstract dashboards.
- State "not a review summarizer, not a CRM" clearly.

### C. Live Demo Interaction

User mindset:

- "Show me quickly."
- "What does this actually produce?"
- "Can I see myself using it?"

Pain felt:

- They have limited patience.
- They need to understand value without setup.

Message they need to hear:

> Load demo data and see this week's revenue actions in under 30 seconds.

Action we want them to take:

- Load demo data.
- Inspect priority actions.
- Copy a sample follow-up.
- Ask to try their own data.

Product asset shown:

- Live demo dashboard.
- Demo dataset selector.
- Revenue Action Board.
- Follow-up message preview.
- Content idea.
- Script suggestion.
- Outcome status.

Conversion goal:

- User clicks or requests free report with their own data.

Failure risk:

- Demo feels like a static sample report.

Mitigation:

- Put action cards first.
- Make the copy action visible.
- Show one clear lead rescue action immediately.

### D. Free Report Request

User mindset:

- "Maybe this could work for my business."
- "I want to see it on my own notes before paying."
- "I am cautious about sharing data."

Pain felt:

- They want value but do not want a heavy commitment.

Message they need to hear:

> Send 10-20 anonymized reviews, inquiries, or sales notes. We will return a free Revenue Action Report showing your top leaks and next actions.

Action we want them to take:

- Submit 10-20 customer voice snippets.
- Provide industry and business context.
- Book or accept a short review call.

Product asset shown:

- Free report form.
- Example input.
- Privacy/anonymization guidance.
- Sample free diagnostic.

Conversion goal:

- Completed free report request with usable data.

Failure risk:

- Users do not submit data.

Mitigation:

- Allow anonymized snippets.
- Give copy/paste examples.
- Offer "send messy notes, we will clean them" positioning.
- Keep the form short.

### E. Free Report Delivery

User mindset:

- "Did this actually find something useful?"
- "Is this worth paying for?"
- "Can I act on this today?"

Pain felt:

- They need quick proof, not a long analysis document.

Message they need to hear:

> These are the three revenue leaks hiding in your customer conversations, and these are the next actions to take.

Action we want them to take:

- Read the diagnostic.
- Choose one action they would use.
- Join a short review call.
- Accept the paid report offer.

Product asset shown:

- 1-page Revenue Leak Diagnostic.
- Top 3 objections.
- Top 3 action opportunities.
- 1 sample follow-up message.
- Paid report preview.

Conversion goal:

- Paid Revenue Action Report offer accepted or serious buying signal.

Failure risk:

- Free report is useful but not urgent enough.

Mitigation:

- Include a "what this is costing you" section.
- Tie one action to a real stale lead or repeated objection.
- Ask what one recovered conversation is worth.

### F. Paid Report Offer

User mindset:

- "This was useful. What do I get if I pay?"
- "Is this worth the price?"
- "Can this help me this week?"

Pain felt:

- They want depth, specificity, and ready-to-use assets.

Message they need to hear:

> The paid Revenue Action Report turns more of your customer conversations into follow-up messages, content ideas, script improvements, and a 7-day action plan.

Action we want them to take:

- Buy the paid Revenue Action Report.
- Submit more data.
- Schedule delivery/review.

Product asset shown:

- Paid report outline.
- Before/after sample.
- Pricing anchor.
- Delivery timeline.

Conversion goal:

- Paid report purchase.

Failure risk:

- Paid report feels too expensive.

Mitigation:

- Anchor against one recovered consultation or deal.
- Show the included assets.
- Offer a clear fixed price.
- Do not pitch subscription first.

### G. Setup Service Upsell

User mindset:

- "The report is useful, but I need help turning this into a system."
- "My data is messy."
- "My team will not maintain this unless it is set up."

Pain felt:

- They lack process, templates, and weekly operating rhythm.

Message they need to hear:

> We can turn your existing lead/review data into an objection taxonomy, follow-up templates, content playbook, and weekly action workflow.

Action we want them to take:

- Buy the done-for-you setup package.
- Share existing lead/review data.
- Commit to a weekly action workflow.

Product asset shown:

- Setup package page.
- Objection taxonomy example.
- Follow-up template pack.
- Content playbook example.
- Weekly workflow checklist.

Conversion goal:

- Setup package purchase.

Failure risk:

- Setup feels labor-heavy or custom consulting.

Mitigation:

- Productize the package.
- Define exact deliverables.
- Limit scope.
- Make setup the bridge to monthly SaaS.

### H. Monthly SaaS Conversion

User mindset:

- "I want this every week."
- "Can I keep refreshing actions as new leads and notes come in?"
- "Can this become part of my operating rhythm?"

Pain felt:

- One report is useful, but revenue leaks keep appearing.

Message they need to hear:

> Keep your customer voice updated and get a weekly Revenue Action Board.

Action we want them to take:

- Subscribe to the monthly plan.
- Add new customer voice weekly.
- Review the board every Monday.

Product asset shown:

- Weekly SaaS plan.
- Action board.
- Outcome tracking.
- Monthly summary report.
- Subscription value comparison.

Conversion goal:

- Monthly subscription.

Failure risk:

- Customer thinks the one-time report is enough.

Mitigation:

- Include 7-day follow-up check.
- Show how new data changes the board.
- Tie subscription to weekly review habit and outcome proof.

### I. Weekly Usage Loop

User mindset:

- "What should I do this week?"
- "What changed since last week?"
- "Which actions should my team execute?"

Pain felt:

- Without habit, the product becomes another unused dashboard.

Message they need to hear:

> Your weekly revenue actions are ready.

Action we want them to take:

- Review actions Monday.
- Send follow-ups Tuesday.
- Publish content Wednesday.
- Improve scripts Thursday.
- Track outcomes Friday.

Product asset shown:

- Weekly Revenue Action Board.
- Follow-up library.
- Content ideas.
- Script suggestions.
- Outcome status.
- Weekly report email.

Conversion goal:

- Weekly active usage.

Failure risk:

- SaaS retention is weak.

Mitigation:

- Tie the product to a weekly operating rhythm.
- Send a weekly report email.
- Make one action easy to execute immediately.
- Keep the first screen action-first.

### J. Outcome Proof

User mindset:

- "Did this work?"
- "Can I justify paying monthly?"
- "Which actions produced replies, bookings, or wins?"

Pain felt:

- They need proof, not just recommendations.

Message they need to hear:

> Track what happened after you acted, so next week's recommendations get sharper.

Action we want them to take:

- Mark actions as copied, sent, replied, booked, won, lost, or ignored.
- Review outcome summary.
- Export/share the report.

Product asset shown:

- Outcome tracking page.
- Outcome summary.
- Estimated recovered revenue.
- Weekly report.
- Case study template.

Conversion goal:

- Recorded outcomes and renewal confidence.

Failure risk:

- Outcome tracking is not used.

Mitigation:

- Keep tracking lightweight.
- Make Friday outcome review part of the usage loop.
- Include outcome summary in reports.
- Ask for outcome data during setup/review calls.

### K. Referral / Expansion

User mindset:

- "This helped me recover conversations."
- "Other consultants or teams could use this."
- "Can I use this for clients?"

Pain felt:

- They want to share proof or apply it to another business unit/client.

Message they need to hear:

> If this helped recover conversations, we can run the same Revenue Action Report for another team, client, or vertical.

Action we want them to take:

- Refer a peer.
- Share a case study.
- Add another client or business line.
- Upgrade to toolkit or multi-client workflow later.

Product asset shown:

- Case study template.
- Referral ask.
- Agency/consultant toolkit preview.
- Before/after proof.

Conversion goal:

- Referral, expansion, or agency/consultant toolkit interest.

Failure risk:

- User sees value but does not talk about it.

Mitigation:

- Give them a simple proof artifact.
- Ask for referral after a recorded win or recovered conversation.
- Make the referral request specific.

## 3. First 10 Seconds

The user must immediately understand:

> Review tools tell you what customers said. Review-to-Revenue AI tells you what to do next.

Within 10 seconds, the page or demo must communicate:

- This is not a review summary tool.
- This is not a CRM.
- The product creates revenue actions.
- The output is practical: contact, send, publish, improve, track.
- The user can try it without setup.

Best first-screen language:

> Turn customer conversations into this week's revenue actions.

Support line:

> Know who to follow up with, what to send, what to publish, what script to improve, and what happened after you acted.

Avoid in first 10 seconds:

- AI-powered insights.
- Review analytics.
- Sentiment dashboard.
- Smart CRM.
- Generic automation.

## 4. First 30 Seconds

The live demo must prove five things:

1. Who to contact.
2. What to send.
3. What to publish.
4. What script to improve.
5. What outcome to track.

Demo proof sequence:

```text
Load demo data
-> See Revenue at Risk
-> See Today's Priority Actions
-> Open one Lead to Rescue
-> Copy follow-up message
-> See content idea
-> See script improvement
-> Mark or view outcome status
```

The user should say:

> I can see what I would do next.

Failure condition:

- User only understands that the product summarizes data.

Mitigation:

- Keep action cards above analysis sections.
- Show follow-up copy preview.
- Show "what to publish" and "what to improve" in the first experience.

## 8. Monthly Retention Loop

Weekly SaaS usage:

```text
Monday: Review Revenue Actions
Tuesday: Send follow-ups
Wednesday: Publish content
Thursday: Improve script
Friday: Track outcomes
```

### Monday: Review Revenue Actions

User action:

- Add new customer voice.
- Review Revenue Action Board.
- Select this week's priorities.

Retention job:

- Make the product the first stop for weekly revenue planning.

### Tuesday: Send Follow-ups

User action:

- Copy messages.
- Send to cold or hesitant leads.
- Mark status as copied or sent.

Retention job:

- Create execution value quickly.

### Wednesday: Publish Content

User action:

- Pick objection-based content.
- Draft or publish.

Retention job:

- Connect customer objections to content production.

### Thursday: Improve Script

User action:

- Replace weak sales language.
- Prepare for calls or consultations.

Retention job:

- Improve future conversations, not only current follow-ups.

### Friday: Track Outcomes

User action:

- Mark replied, booked, won, lost, or ignored.
- Export or review weekly summary.

Retention job:

- Close the loop and make next week smarter.

Retention principle:

> The product retains when it becomes a weekly revenue habit, not when it produces a one-time insight.

## 11. Metrics

Track funnel metrics:

- Visitors.
- Demo clicks.
- Free report requests.
- Reports delivered.
- Paid report conversion.
- Setup conversion.
- SaaS conversion.
- Weekly active users.
- Actions sent.
- Outcomes recorded.
- Retained accounts.
- Referrals.

Metric definitions:

Visitors:

- Unique landing page visitors.

Demo clicks:

- Users who start or interact with the live demo.

Free report requests:

- Users who submit usable contact and customer voice data.

Reports delivered:

- Free diagnostics or paid reports delivered.

Paid report conversion:

- Free diagnostic recipients who buy a Revenue Action Report.

Setup conversion:

- Paid report buyers who buy done-for-you setup.

SaaS conversion:

- Customers who subscribe monthly.

Weekly active users:

- Accounts that review actions or update data during a week.

Actions sent:

- Actions marked copied or sent.

Outcomes recorded:

- Actions marked replied, booked, won, lost, or ignored.

Retained accounts:

- Accounts active and paying after 30, 60, or 90 days.

Referrals:

- New prospects introduced by an existing user or customer.

North star metric:

> Revenue actions executed with outcome recorded.

Why:

- It combines action and learning.
- It avoids vanity analytics.
- It supports the data flywheel.

## 12. Risks

### Risk 1: Users do not submit data.

Why:

- Data feels sensitive or messy.

Mitigation:

- Allow anonymized snippets.
- Provide example inputs.
- Accept messy notes.
- Offer done-for-you cleanup.
- Keep the free report form short.

### Risk 2: Free report does not convert.

Why:

- Free report may be useful but not urgent.

Mitigation:

- Tie report to stale leads and specific revenue leaks.
- Include one concrete follow-up message.
- Make the paid report a clear expansion, not a vague upsell.

### Risk 3: Paid report feels too expensive.

Why:

- Customer may compare it to free AI tools.

Mitigation:

- Anchor price to one recovered conversation or consultation.
- Show deliverables.
- Use evidence-backed recommendations.
- Avoid generic AI copy.

### Risk 4: Setup service is labor-heavy.

Why:

- Early customers may need data cleanup and custom playbooks.

Mitigation:

- Productize deliverables.
- Limit scope.
- Reuse templates.
- Convert repeated service work into product requirements.

### Risk 5: SaaS retention is weak.

Why:

- Users may not build a weekly habit.

Mitigation:

- Use Monday-Friday retention loop.
- Send weekly report email.
- Make one action executable quickly.
- Track outcomes and show weekly value.

### Risk 6: Outcome tracking is not used.

Why:

- Users may forget or not see value.

Mitigation:

- Keep status options simple.
- Tie outcome tracking to Friday review.
- Include outcome summary in reports.
- Ask for outcomes in review calls.

### Risk 7: Landing page feels generic.

Why:

- Category language may sound like broad AI software.

Mitigation:

- Lead with the comparison line.
- Use specific Franchise / Business Consulting examples.
- Show concrete action cards and sample report sections.
- Avoid generic AI phrases.
