# Category Design

## Purpose

This document decides the category Review-to-Revenue AI belongs to or creates before the Master PRD is written.

The goal is to stop the product from drifting between review analyzer, CRM, reputation tool, dashboard, AI writer, or generic report generator.

Product name and category must be separate:

- Product name: **Review-to-Revenue AI**
- Customer-facing category: **Revenue Action Platform**
- Investor-facing category phrase: **Revenue Intelligence Layer for Customer Voice**

## 1. Category Definition

Recommended category:

> Revenue Action Platform

Definition:

> A Revenue Action Platform turns unstructured customer voice into prioritized revenue actions and tracks what happened after the business acted.

Expanded definition:

> A Revenue Action Platform reads reviews, inquiries, sales notes, competitor complaints, lead data, and other customer language to identify revenue signals, generate weekly actions, and build outcome data that improves future recommendations.

This category is built around the product loop:

```text
Customer Voice
-> Revenue Signal
-> Revenue Action
-> Outcome Data
-> Better Recommendations
```

The category is not defined by data source. It is defined by the job:

> Help the business know what revenue action to take this week.

## 2. Why Existing Categories Are Not Enough

### Review Management

Review management tools collect, monitor, organize, and reply to reviews.

Why this is not enough:

- Reviews are only one customer voice source.
- The main user need is not another review feed.
- Review summaries rarely become follow-up messages, content topics, sales scripts, or outcome-tracked actions.
- Review management stops too close to public reputation and too far from revenue execution.

### CRM

CRM tools store contacts, lead records, notes, deals, tasks, and pipeline stages.

Why this is not enough:

- CRM tells the team where a lead sits.
- It often does not explain why the lead stalled.
- It does not automatically turn messy customer language into the next message, content idea, script improvement, or objection-handling move.
- CRM is a system of record; Review-to-Revenue AI is an action-intelligence layer.

### Reputation Management

Reputation management tools improve public visibility, ratings, listings, and brand trust.

Why this is not enough:

- Reputation management is outward-facing.
- The revenue problem is often private: a lead hesitated, compared, stopped replying, or did not trust the offer.
- Reputation tools optimize public perception, but they do not primarily run the weekly revenue action loop.

### CX Platform

CX platforms capture feedback, analyze customer experience, report trends, and support enterprise experience programs.

Why this is not enough:

- CX platforms are often broad, enterprise-oriented, and dashboard-heavy.
- They help teams understand experience, but the workflow can stop at insight, reporting, or operational improvement.
- Smaller revenue teams need direct action: who to contact, what to send, what to publish, what to improve, and what happened after acting.

### AI Sales Assistant

AI sales assistants draft emails, summarize calls, qualify leads, automate prospecting, or assist reps in an existing workflow.

Why this is not enough:

- They usually start after a lead or sales task is already defined.
- They may write faster, but they do not necessarily decide what customer voice pattern should shape the action.
- Generic AI writing is easy to copy.
- The durable category is not "AI writes sales messages." It is "customer voice becomes revenue action and outcome learning."

## 3. Category Options Evaluated

### 1. Revenue Action Platform

Recommendation: **Use as the primary customer-facing category.**

Strengths:

- Easy to understand.
- Directly communicates action instead of analysis.
- Avoids sounding like CRM, CX, or reputation management.
- Works for landing pages, demos, product navigation, and sales calls.
- Matches the current MVP: revenue actions, follow-up messages, content ideas, script improvements, competitor weakness, and outcome tracking.

Weaknesses:

- It may sound operational before it sounds intelligent.
- Investors may ask whether this is a feature, workflow, or standalone platform.

How to use:

- Landing page category.
- Product explanation.
- Demo framing.
- Customer-facing sales language.

### 2. Customer Voice Revenue Intelligence

Recommendation: **Use as supporting strategic language, not the primary category.**

Strengths:

- Communicates the data source and strategic value.
- Stronger than "review analytics."
- Helpful for investor and strategy docs.

Weaknesses:

- Slightly awkward as a category name.
- Less natural in a landing-page headline.
- Customers may understand "action" faster than "intelligence."

How to use:

- Investor narrative.
- Category education.
- Product strategy documents.

### 3. Revenue Intelligence Layer for Customer Voice

Recommendation: **Use as the investor-facing category phrase.**

Strengths:

- Clearly positions the product as a layer on top of CRMs, review tools, spreadsheets, and sales notes.
- Avoids claiming to replace existing systems.
- Explains why the product can coexist with incumbents.
- Stronger for fundraising because it points toward a data flywheel and platform expansion.

Weaknesses:

- Too abstract for small business customers.
- Too long for a product page hero.

How to use:

- Pitch deck.
- Investor memo.
- Strategic narrative.
- Partnership conversations.

### 4. RevenueOS

Recommendation: **Reserve for future only. Do not use now.**

Strengths:

- Ambitious.
- Suggests a broad operating layer for revenue teams.

Weaknesses:

- Too broad for the current MVP.
- Sounds like CRM, sales operating system, or all-in-one revenue platform.
- Creates expectations around auth, database, team workflows, integrations, automations, forecasting, and reporting.
- Weakens the current wedge by making the product look heavier than it is.

How to use:

- Do not use in current customer-facing or investor-facing language.
- Revisit only if the product expands into a true operating system after integrations and workflow ownership are proven.

### 5. Review-to-Revenue AI as a Product Name Only

Recommendation: **Yes. Keep as the product name, not the category.**

Strengths:

- Memorable transformation.
- Clear contrast against review summarizers.
- Already consistent with the codebase and current docs.
- Works well as an MVP product name.

Weaknesses:

- "Review" may sound narrower than customer voice.
- The name alone should not carry the entire category story.

How to use:

- Product name.
- Logo/name lockup.
- Demo title.
- Report title.

How not to use:

- Do not call the category "Review-to-Revenue."
- Do not let the product sound like a review-only tool.

## 5. Why This Category Is Easier To Understand For Customers

Customers do not wake up wanting customer voice intelligence.

They wake up asking:

- Who should I follow up with?
- What should I send?
- What objection keeps blocking deals?
- What content should I publish?
- What script should my team improve?
- Did any of this create replies, bookings, or deals?

"Revenue Action Platform" is easier because:

- "Revenue" names the business outcome.
- "Action" names the immediate job.
- "Platform" gives room to grow without sounding like a simple report generator.
- It does not require the customer to learn a new analytics concept.
- It makes the first screen intuitive: this week's revenue actions.

Customer-facing category sentence:

> A Revenue Action Platform shows what to do next with the customer voice you already have.

## 6. Why This Category Is Stronger For Investors

"Revenue Action Platform" is customer-clear, but investors also need to understand scale and defensibility.

The investor-strength phrase is:

> Revenue Intelligence Layer for Customer Voice

Why this is stronger for investors:

- It positions the product as a layer, not a point tool.
- It explains why the product can sit on top of CRMs, review tools, spreadsheets, and communication platforms.
- It points toward a data flywheel: customer voice, recommended action, outcome data, better recommendation.
- It supports vertical expansion through industry playbooks.
- It makes the product bigger than review management without claiming to replace CRM.

Investor-facing category sentence:

> Review-to-Revenue AI is a revenue intelligence layer for customer voice, turning messy customer language into action data and outcome learning across high-consideration service businesses.

## 9. Naming Decision

### Keep Review-to-Revenue AI now?

Decision: **Yes.**

Keep `Review-to-Revenue AI` as the product name for the MVP and first validation cycles.

Why:

- It is already aligned with the current codebase and docs.
- It clearly says the product converts feedback into commercial value.
- It is sharper than a generic "Customer Signal" name.
- It creates contrast with review summarizers.

Condition:

- Every major message must explain that "review" is one customer voice source, not the whole product.

### Use Revenue Action Platform as category?

Decision: **Yes.**

Use `Revenue Action Platform` as the primary category.

Why:

- It is customer-friendly.
- It points to the weekly workflow.
- It avoids CRM/reputation/CX confusion.
- It keeps the product focused on action and outcome.

Best phrase:

> Revenue Action Platform for Customer Voice

### Reserve Voice-to-Revenue for future?

Decision: **Yes.**

Reserve `Voice-to-Revenue` as a future rename candidate only if the product clearly expands beyond review-led positioning.

Use later if:

- Most validated input comes from calls, consult notes, DMs, forms, lead CSVs, and sales notes rather than public reviews.
- Customers naturally describe the product as "customer voice to revenue."
- The `Review-to-Revenue` name becomes a growth constraint.

### Reserve RevenueOS for future?

Decision: **Yes, but do not use soon.**

Reserve `RevenueOS` only for a much later stage.

Use later if:

- The product owns team workflows.
- It integrates with CRM and messaging systems.
- It manages tasks, outcomes, playbooks, reports, and recommendations at organization scale.

Do not use now because:

- It sounds too broad.
- It implies CRM replacement.
- It weakens the current wedge.

## 10. Moat

The moat is not a single AI feature.

The moat is the compounding loop between customer voice, revenue action, outcome data, industry playbooks, and better recommendations.

### Customer Voice

Customer voice is the raw material.

Sources include:

- Reviews
- Inquiries
- Sales notes
- Consultation notes
- Competitor complaints
- DMs
- Call notes
- Lead CSVs
- Survey comments

Moat contribution:

- The product learns the language of hesitation, trust, proof, risk, urgency, and buying intent.
- Over time, vertical-specific language patterns become harder to copy than generic summarization.

### Revenue Action

Revenue action is the product unit.

Action types include:

- Follow-up message
- Content recommendation
- Sales script improvement
- Competitor weakness positioning
- Lead rescue opportunity
- Trust asset recommendation

Moat contribution:

- Actions are closer to business value than summaries.
- A product built around action can prove value faster than analytics dashboards.

### Outcome Data

Outcome data closes the loop.

Tracked outcomes include:

- Copied
- Sent
- Replied
- Booked
- Won
- Lost
- Ignored
- Estimated recovered revenue

Moat contribution:

- Outcome data teaches which actions worked.
- Competitors can generate suggestions, but suggestions without outcome learning become generic.

### Industry Playbooks

Industry playbooks turn the platform from generic AI into vertical intelligence.

Examples:

- Franchise consulting: investment amount, payback period, family approval, brand comparison, location risk.
- Clinics / med spas: price, downtime, proof, risk, expectations, spouse approval.
- Wedding vendors: package clarity, partner approval, proof, timeline, hidden fees.
- Home remodeling: quote trust, timeline risk, proof, materials, budget confidence.

Moat contribution:

- Vertical playbooks improve output quality and reduce generic AI feel.
- They create faster time-to-value for each market.

### Better Recommendations

Better recommendations are the compounding result.

The system improves when it can connect:

```text
Customer language pattern
-> Recommended action
-> User execution
-> Outcome
-> Future recommendation
```

Moat contribution:

- This creates a practical data flywheel.
- The product becomes more valuable as it sees which language, actions, and playbooks produce replies, bookings, and wins.
