## 6. Customer Interview Script

### Interview Goal

Find evidence. Do not sell. Do not lead the witness.

Test:

- data willingness
- pain intensity
- report value
- willingness to pay
- ChatGPT alternative
- action execution
- outcome tracking

### Opening

Thanks for taking the time.

I am not trying to pitch you a finished product today. I am trying to understand whether teams like yours have a real problem around lost leads, customer objections, and follow-up execution.

Some questions may sound direct because I am trying to avoid building something nobody needs.

### Context Questions

1. What kind of customer inquiries or consultation conversations do you handle most often?
2. Where do those conversations usually come from?
3. What happens after a first inquiry or consultation?
4. Where do leads most often go quiet?
5. How do you currently record sales notes or customer questions?

### Pain Intensity

6. Can you describe the last time a promising lead went cold?
7. What do you think caused that lead to stop responding?
8. How often does that happen in a normal week?
9. What does a lost qualified lead cost you?
10. What have you already tried to fix this?

### Data Willingness

11. What kind of customer conversations or sales notes would you be comfortable sharing if they were anonymized?
12. What would make you unwilling to share them?
13. Who would need to approve sharing anonymized notes?
14. Would pasting 10-20 anonymized snippets be easy or annoying?
15. What format would be easiest for you: pasted text, CSV, document, screenshots, or call notes?

### Report Value

16. If someone reviewed your notes and identified the top 3 revenue leaks, what would make that useful?
17. What would make the report useless?
18. Which output would you care about most: follow-up messages, content ideas, script improvements, lead rescue list, or outcome plan?
19. What would you expect to see before trusting the recommendation?
20. What would make you send the recommendation to your team?

### Willingness To Pay

21. Have you paid for consulting, sales scripts, CRM setup, or marketing audits before?
22. What did you pay for, and what made it worth it or not worth it?
23. If a one-time report helped recover one serious conversation, what would that be worth?
24. At what price would a paid Revenue Action Report feel too expensive to test?
25. At what price would it feel suspiciously cheap?

### ChatGPT Alternative

26. Have you tried using ChatGPT on sales notes, reviews, or customer messages?
27. What worked?
28. What did not work?
29. What would a product or service need to do better than a prompt?
30. Would you rather use a prompt yourself or receive a structured report? Why?

### Action Execution

31. When you receive a recommendation, who actually executes it?
32. What usually stops follow-up actions from happening?
33. Would you send one suggested follow-up message to test this?
34. Would you publish one content idea based on a real objection?
35. Would you change one line in your consultation script?

### Outcome Tracking

36. How do you currently know whether a follow-up worked?
37. Would you be willing to record reply, booked, won, lost, or no response?
38. What would make outcome tracking too much work?
39. Who should own outcome tracking?
40. What would make you come back weekly?

### Closing

41. What part of this feels most valuable?
42. What part feels unnecessary?
43. What would you delete?
44. What would you need to see before paying?
45. Who else should I talk to?

### Questions To Avoid

Avoid:

- Would you use this?
- Do you like this idea?
- Would AI help your sales?
- Would you pay for this someday?
- Is this a good product?

Ask instead:

- What did you do last time this happened?
- What did you pay for before?
- What would you send me today?
- What would make this useless?
- What action would you actually take this week?

## 10. Objection Handling Library

### "Can't I just use ChatGPT?"

Yes, maybe.

That is part of what this validation needs to prove.

If ChatGPT gives you the same level of clarity from your notes, you should not pay for this.

The difference we are testing is whether a structured diagnostic can connect real customer language to revenue leaks, follow-up actions, content, script changes, and outcome tracking faster than doing it yourself with prompts.

The test is simple: send anonymized notes, take one action, and see if it creates a reply, booking, or useful learning.

### "I don't have clean data."

Clean data is not required.

Messy sales notes, copied inquiry messages, call summaries, or repeated objections are enough for the diagnostic.

If the data is too messy, the report will say so clearly and mark confidence as low.

### "I don't want to upload private data."

Do not upload private data.

Remove names, phone numbers, emails, company names, and any sensitive details.

For this validation, pasted anonymized snippets are enough. The point is to understand customer language, not identify customers.

### "I don't need another tool."

That makes sense.

This is not asking you to adopt another tool right now.

The first step is a one-time diagnostic. The only question is whether your existing customer conversations contain follow-up actions worth testing this week.

### "Will this actually increase sales?"

Not guaranteed.

The honest claim is smaller: this should identify one or two actions worth testing against real leads or real objections.

The validation is whether those actions create replies, bookings, won deals, better lost reasons, or clearer next steps.

### "I'm too busy."

That is exactly why the first ask is small.

Send 10-20 anonymized snippets without cleaning them. The diagnostic comes back with the top leaks and one recommended action.

If even that feels too heavy, send 5 snippets first and we will decide whether there is enough signal.

## 11. 14-Day Execution Plan

### Day 1

- Who to contact: Build a list of 50 franchise / business consulting operators, consultants, advisors, and agencies.
- What to send: Do not send yet. Segment the list and personalize the first 20.
- Evidence to collect: Prospect list quality, segment notes, likely data owner.
- Success means: 50 relevant prospects identified.
- Failure means: list is too broad, unclear buyer, or no direct access to operators.

### Day 2

- Who to contact: First 20 highest-fit prospects.
- What to send: Founder-led outreach or cold email.
- Evidence to collect: opens if available, replies, confusion, data willingness.
- Success means: 2-4 replies or clear objections.
- Failure means: no replies and no learning from message.

### Day 3

- Who to contact: Next 20 prospects and Day 2 responders.
- What to send: Cold email or DM. Reply manually to any response.
- Evidence to collect: privacy concerns, data format concerns, pain statements.
- Success means: at least 1 serious conversation scheduled or data submission promised.
- Failure means: prospects do not understand the offer.

### Day 4

- Who to contact: Final 10 prospects plus warm contacts.
- What to send: Short DM version.
- Evidence to collect: fastest path to data, reasons for refusal.
- Success means: 50 total outreach attempts completed.
- Failure means: fewer than 50 attempts or no clear objection pattern.

### Day 5

- Who to contact: Prospects who replied or showed interest.
- What to send: Interview request and data request.
- Evidence to collect: pain intensity, willingness to share anonymized notes.
- Success means: 3-5 real conversations booked.
- Failure means: people are curious but will not share data or talk.

### Day 6

- Who to contact: Prospects who promised data but have not sent it.
- What to send: Data reminder with examples of acceptable anonymized notes.
- Evidence to collect: friction in submitting data.
- Success means: first 1-2 data submissions received.
- Failure means: nobody sends actual notes.

### Day 7

- Who to contact: First data submitters.
- What to send: Free Revenue Leak Diagnostic.
- Evidence to collect: which sections they read, trust level, usefulness.
- Success means: at least 1 diagnostic delivered and reviewed.
- Failure means: report does not create interest or action.

### Day 8

- Who to contact: Diagnostic recipients.
- What to send: Review call request and one recommended action.
- Evidence to collect: action willingness, strongest report section, weak sections.
- Success means: recipient agrees to test one follow-up, content, or script action.
- Failure means: they say it is interesting but take no action.

### Day 9

- Who to contact: Strong-fit diagnostic recipients.
- What to send: Paid Revenue Action Report offer.
- Evidence to collect: price reaction, buying objection, perceived value.
- Success means: 1-2 paid offers seriously considered.
- Failure means: no one sees enough value to consider paying.

### Day 10

- Who to contact: Additional data submitters and warm prospects.
- What to send: More diagnostics or data reminders.
- Evidence to collect: whether repeatable delivery is possible.
- Success means: 3 free diagnostics delivered total.
- Failure means: diagnostics take too long or rely on too much custom thinking.

### Day 11

- Who to contact: People who agreed to take action.
- What to send: Outcome tracking reminder.
- Evidence to collect: action executed, message sent, content published, script changed.
- Success means: 2 users execute recommended actions.
- Failure means: people agree in theory but do not act.

### Day 12

- Who to contact: Interview participants.
- What to send: ChatGPT comparison question.
- Evidence to collect: why they would or would not use ChatGPT instead.
- Success means: clear differentiation need appears.
- Failure means: prospects believe a prompt fully solves the problem.

### Day 13

- Who to contact: Action executors.
- What to send: Outcome check.
- Evidence to collect: reply, booked call, won/lost status, no response, learning.
- Success means: 1 outcome recorded.
- Failure means: no one tracks or reports outcomes.

### Day 14

- Who to contact: All active prospects with open loops.
- What to send: final check-in, paid report close, or next-step request.
- Evidence to collect: paid conversion, strong verbal commitment, no-go reasons.
- Success means: minimum evidence threshold is met or nearly met.
- Failure means: no data, no payment signal, no action, or no outcome.

## 12. Kill Criteria Reminder

Product build remains No-Go unless evidence passes.

Minimum signals required:

- 50 outreach attempts
- 10 real conversations
- 5 data submissions
- 3 free diagnostics delivered
- 2 paid report offers seriously considered
- 1 paid conversion or very strong verbal commitment
- 2 users execute recommended actions
- 1 outcome recorded

### If Minimum Signals Do Not Happen

Do not build more product.

Decide whether to:

- narrow the segment
- change the offer
- change the data request
- change the report format
- change the buyer
- stop the project

### What Does Not Count As Evidence

- compliments
- "interesting idea"
- likes on posts
- generic AI curiosity
- people saying they would use it someday
- unpaid enthusiasm with no data shared
- report downloads with no action taken
