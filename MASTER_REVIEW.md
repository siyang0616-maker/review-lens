# MASTER REVIEW

Stage 5A: Master Review & Assumption Challenge

## Executive Summary

Brutal verdict:

> No-Go on building more product right now.

The strategy has a real insight: customer language can reveal revenue actions. But the company is still making too many claims before evidence exists. The documents say the product is not a review analyzer, not a CRM, not a dashboard, and not a report generator. Yet the go-to-market still depends on reports, the product still centers a dashboard, the data model still touches CRM-like statuses, and the name still says "Review."

The strongest version of the company is not a broad Revenue Action Platform yet. It is a founder-led validation service for one narrow market:

> Franchise / business consultants with stale leads, repeated objections, and messy sales notes.

The next stage should not be a Master PRD for product build. It should be an evidence sprint that proves people will share data, pay for a report, execute the recommendations, and report outcomes. Without that evidence, the current plan risks becoming a polished SaaS wrapper around unvalidated consulting logic.

## How Competitors Would Beat Us

### How ReviewPro Would Beat Us

ReviewPro would beat us in hospitality by using existing hotel relationships, guest feedback aggregation, sentiment, competitor benchmarking, surveys, case management, reporting, and Shiji's broader hotel technology footprint. It could add a "weekly revenue action" layer on top of review feedback and sell it as a feature to hotels already using its platform.

ReviewPro's attack:

- "You already manage guest feedback here. Why export data to another tool?"
- "We already know hotel reputation, languages, sources, benchmarks, and operations."
- "We can connect action recommendations to hotel case management and property workflows."
- "We have trust, integrations, and hospitality-specific compliance."

Our only defense:

- Do not start in hospitality as the first commercial wedge.
- Keep hotels as demo/later vertical until the action workflow is proven elsewhere.

### How HubSpot Would Beat Us

HubSpot would beat us by embedding customer voice analysis directly into CRM records, deals, tasks, notes, marketing content, sequences, and AI assistants. It already owns the contact, deal, pipeline, task, reporting, and integration layer.

HubSpot's attack:

- "Your leads and notes are already in HubSpot."
- "We can analyze stalled deals and generate next steps without switching tools."
- "We can connect actions to email, tasks, marketing automation, reporting, and revenue attribution."
- "We can distribute this through a massive existing customer base."

Our only defense:

- Position as a lightweight pre-CRM or alongside-CRM action service for customers who do not use HubSpot deeply.
- Use vertical playbooks and manual service insight before HubSpot would care.

### How Podium Would Beat Us

Podium would beat us by owning local business messaging, AI lead response, appointment booking, dormant customer reactivation, review workflows, and revenue claims. It is closer to execution than most competitors because it can actually send, respond, qualify, and book.

Podium's attack:

- "Why generate a follow-up message elsewhere when our AI can respond instantly?"
- "We already reduce missed opportunities through messaging automation."
- "We already serve home services, aesthetics, retail, and auto."
- "We connect lead response to booked appointments and revenue."

Our only defense:

- Avoid competing on messaging automation.
- Focus on upstream diagnosis: what objections, content, and scripts should shape the weekly revenue work.

### How Birdeye Would Beat Us

Birdeye would beat us by adding action recommendations to its existing review, reputation, listings, social, referral, messaging, and multi-location marketing platform. It already has enterprise/multi-location credibility and a broad marketing surface.

Birdeye's attack:

- "We already manage reviews, reputation, listings, social, and customer messaging."
- "We can recommend content and actions from the data already in the platform."
- "Multi-location brands do not want another lightweight tool."
- "We can bundle this into existing agentic marketing workflows."

Our only defense:

- Start with founder-led high-consideration service businesses that are not Birdeye's core enterprise buyer.
- Win on specificity, not platform breadth.

## Evidence Needed Before Building More

Before writing a Master PRD for implementation, the company needs evidence in these areas:

### Customer Pain Evidence

- 20 direct conversations with the first beachhead.
- At least 10 prospects describing the same painful follow-up or objection problem without being prompted.
- Clear proof that stale leads, weak follow-up, or repeated objections create weekly pain.

### Data Access Evidence

- At least 8 prospects willing to share 10-20 anonymized notes, inquiries, or lead snippets.
- At least 5 submissions with data rich enough to generate useful actions.
- Documentation of why prospects refuse to share data.

### Value Evidence

- At least 5 free diagnostics where the customer says, "I would use this action."
- At least 3 cases where the customer sends a recommended follow-up or uses a script/content recommendation.
- At least 2 cases with a recorded reply, booking, or meaningful sales movement.

### Payment Evidence

- At least 5 paid report offers made at a real price.
- At least 2 paid reports purchased.
- At least 1 buyer who asks for ongoing weekly help or setup.

### Retention Evidence

- At least 3 customers willing to repeat the workflow after 7-14 days.
- At least 2 customers willing to report outcomes after acting.
- Proof that new data changes what they should do next.

### Positioning Evidence

- Test whether customers understand:
  - "Turn customer voice into revenue actions."
  - "Revenue Action Platform."
  - "Review-to-Revenue AI."
  - "Free Revenue Leak Diagnostic."
- Track which phrase creates the most qualified data submissions.

## If We Only Had 90 Days And $0 Marketing Budget

Do not build a bigger app.

Build only what helps sell and deliver the first paid proof:

1. A one-page landing page or simple public page for one audience: franchise / business consultants.
2. A free diagnostic intake form asking for 10-20 anonymized notes.
3. A manual Revenue Leak Diagnostic template.
4. A paid Revenue Action Report template.
5. A narrow Korean franchise consulting demo dataset.
6. A simple outcome follow-up sheet.
7. A case study template.
8. A founder-led outreach script.
9. A 20-minute diagnostic review call script.
10. A hard validation dashboard tracking interviews, data submissions, paid offers, paid conversions, actions executed, and outcomes recorded.

### 90-Day Plan

Days 1-10:

- Identify 50 franchise / business consulting prospects.
- Conduct 15-20 interviews.
- Ask for anonymized customer notes.
- Test whether the pain appears without prompting.

Days 11-30:

- Deliver 8-10 free diagnostics manually.
- Ask every recipient which action they would actually take.
- Make 5-8 paid report offers.
- Do not ask if they like the idea. Ask if they will pay.

Days 31-45:

- Sell at least 2 paid Revenue Action Reports.
- Deliver manually.
- Track how much work is repeatable versus custom.
- Ask customers to execute one action within 7 days.

Days 46-60:

- Run outcome follow-ups.
- Identify whether actions created replies, bookings, or stronger sales conversations.
- Offer setup only to customers who executed actions.

Days 61-90:

- Test ongoing weekly action refresh with 2-3 customers.
- Do not call it SaaS unless they use it weekly.
- Decide whether to build product, stay service-led, pivot vertical, or stop.

### 90-Day Success Criteria

Continue only if:

- 8+ prospects share usable data.
- 2+ customers buy paid reports.
- 1+ customer buys setup or requests recurring weekly help.
- 2+ customers execute at least one recommended action.
- 1+ credible outcome is recorded.

### 90-Day Failure Criteria

Stop or reposition if:

- Prospects do not share data.
- Reports are liked but not bought.
- Buyers do not execute recommendations.
- No one reports outcomes.
- Customers only want generic ChatGPT prompts.
- The founder must do too much custom analysis for every delivery.
